NEPSE IPO vs Secondary Market Nepal 2026: Which is Better for Investors?

📅 Published: June 22, 2026 ⏱️ Reading Time: 10 minutes 👤 NEPSE Calculator Nepal 📧 nepsecalcnepal@gmail.com

Introduction: Two Ways to Invest in Nepal's Stock Market

When you want to invest in Nepal's stock market, you have two main options: apply for an IPO (Initial Public Offering) or buy shares directly on the secondary market through NEPSE. Both have their own advantages, risks, and processes.

Many beginners in Nepal ask: "Should I apply for IPOs or buy shares on NEPSE?" The answer depends on your goals, risk tolerance, and investment style. This guide breaks down everything you need to know to make the right choice.

💡 Quick Answer: IPOs are lower risk and often give quick profits on listing day. Secondary market gives you more control, flexibility and bigger long-term gains — but requires more knowledge and carries more risk.

What is an IPO in Nepal?

An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a company offers its shares to the general public for the first time. In Nepal, IPOs are regulated by SEBON and applications are submitted through the ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) system via your bank or broker.

  • ✅ You apply for a fixed number of shares at a fixed price (usually Rs. 100 face value)
  • ✅ If allotted, shares are credited to your Demat account
  • ✅ You can sell on listing day or hold long-term
  • ✅ No brokerage, SEBON fee or DP charge on application — only on selling
  • ⚠️ Allotment is not guaranteed — it depends on demand and lottery

What is the Secondary Market in Nepal?

The secondary market is the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) where already-listed shares are bought and sold between investors. You trade through your broker's TMS (Trading Management System) platform.

  • ✅ Buy and sell any listed share anytime during market hours
  • ✅ Choose exactly how many shares at what price
  • ✅ Use limit orders or market orders
  • ⚠️ Brokerage, SEBON fee, DP charge and CGT all apply
  • ⚠️ Prices fluctuate — you can lose money if price falls

IPO Process in Nepal: Step by Step

  1. Check for open IPOs on NEPSE website or your broker's app
  2. Log in to your bank's internet banking or MeroShare
  3. Go to ASBA → Apply for IPO
  4. Enter company name, number of shares (minimum 10), and your BOID (Demat number)
  5. Amount is blocked in your account (not deducted yet)
  6. Wait for allotment result (usually 1–2 weeks)
  7. If allotted: amount deducted, shares credited to your Demat
  8. If not allotted: blocked amount released back to your account
  9. Wait for listing day → shares appear on NEPSE
  10. Sell on listing day or hold for long-term

✅ Good news: You can apply for multiple IPOs simultaneously using the same bank account. Your money is only blocked, not spent, until allotment.

Advantages and Disadvantages

🏦 IPO — Pros & Cons

  • ✅ Low risk — fixed price
  • ✅ No charges on application
  • ✅ Minimum Rs. 1,000 needed
  • ✅ Often lists above issue price
  • ✅ No stock analysis needed
  • ❌ Allotment not guaranteed
  • ❌ Capital locked during waiting period
  • ❌ Limited shares available
  • ❌ Some IPOs list below issue price

📈 Secondary Market — Pros & Cons

  • ✅ Buy anytime, any quantity
  • ✅ Huge selection of stocks
  • ✅ Can earn big long-term gains
  • ✅ Can set target buy price
  • ✅ No waiting for allotment
  • ❌ Brokerage + SEBON on buy AND sell
  • ❌ Price can fall significantly
  • ❌ Requires stock analysis knowledge
  • ❌ Emotional decisions lead to losses

Which is Better for You?

💡 Best Strategy: Most successful Nepal investors do both — apply for all IPOs (low risk, free to try) AND hold quality stocks in the secondary market for long-term growth.

Common Mistakes Nepal Investors Make

  • ❌ Selling IPO shares immediately on listing day — sometimes holding 6–12 months gives much better returns
  • ❌ Applying for IPO of every company without research — some companies list below issue price
  • ❌ Buying secondary market shares based on tips — always do your own research
  • ❌ Panic selling when price drops — short-term drops are normal for good companies
  • ❌ Not tracking charges — brokerage + SEBON + CGT significantly reduce your actual profit
  • ❌ Investing money you need soon — stock market is for money you can lock for at least 1–2 years

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Can I apply for an IPO without a Demat account?

No. You must have a Demat account (BOID number) to apply for any IPO in Nepal. You can open one through your broker or CDS & Clearing Ltd.

Q2: How many IPO shares can I apply for?

Minimum is 10 shares per application. Maximum varies by company and is mentioned in the IPO prospectus. Most retail investors are limited to a certain maximum per individual.

Q3: Can I apply for the same IPO from multiple bank accounts?

No. Each individual (identified by their citizenship number) can apply for one IPO only once. Applying multiple times from different banks for the same IPO is not allowed.

Q4: What happens if an IPO lists below issue price?

You can either sell at a loss on listing day, or hold and wait for the price to recover. This is why researching the company before applying is important — not all IPOs are profitable.

Q5: How long does it take for IPO shares to list on NEPSE?

Typically 2–4 weeks after the IPO closes. The exact listing date is announced by NEPSE and the company. You can track it on the NEPSE website.

Q6: Is CGT charged on IPO profits?

Yes. When you sell your IPO shares in the secondary market (after listing), CGT applies on the profit. If you sell within 365 days of receiving the shares, it's 7.5%. After 365 days, it's 5%.

Q7: Which is safer — IPO or secondary market?

IPO is generally considered safer for beginners because the issue price is fixed (usually Rs. 100) and shares rarely list below this. Secondary market prices fluctuate daily and carry higher risk of loss.
