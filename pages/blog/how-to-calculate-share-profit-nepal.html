How to Calculate Share Profit in Nepal 2026: Complete Step-by-Step Guide

📅 Published: June 16, 2026 ⏱️ Reading Time: 10 minutes 👤 NEPSE Calculator Nepal 📧 nepsecalcnepal@gmail.com

Why Calculating Share Profit is Not Simple in Nepal

Many NEPSE investors make the mistake of calculating profit as simply Sell Price − Buy Price. But this gives you the wrong number every time.

In Nepal's stock market, every trade involves multiple charges — brokerage commission, SEBON fee, DP charge, and Capital Gains Tax. All of these eat into your profit. If you ignore them, you will always overestimate your actual returns.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Bought at Rs. 500, sold at Rs. 700 — many investors think profit = Rs. 200 per share. The actual net profit is less after all charges are deducted.

This guide will teach you the exact formula used by professional NEPSE investors to calculate true net profit.

All Charges You Must Include

Before calculating profit, you must know every charge involved in a NEPSE trade:

Charge Rate On Buy? On Sell?
Brokerage Commission 0.24% – 0.36% (tiered) ✅ Yes ✅ Yes
SEBON Fee 0.015% ✅ Yes ✅ Yes
DP Charge Rs. 25 flat ❌ No ✅ Yes
Capital Gains Tax 5% or 7.5% on profit ❌ No ✅ Yes (if profit)

The Complete Formula (Step by Step)

1

Calculate Total Buy Cost

Buy Amount + Brokerage on Buy + SEBON Fee on Buy

2

Calculate Total Sell Revenue

Sell Amount − Brokerage on Sell − SEBON Fee on Sell − DP Charge

3

Calculate Raw Profit

Sell Amount − Buy Amount (used only for CGT calculation)

4

Calculate Capital Gains Tax

If Raw Profit > 0: CGT = Raw Profit × CGT Rate (5% or 7.5%). If loss: CGT = Rs. 0

5

Calculate Net Profit

Net Sell Revenue − CGT − Total Buy Cost

— COMPLETE FORMULA — Total Buy Cost = (Shares × Buy Price) + Brokerage(Buy) + SEBON(Buy) Total Sell Revenue= (Shares × Sell Price) − Brokerage(Sell) − SEBON(Sell) − DP(Rs.25) Raw Profit = (Shares × Sell Price) − (Shares × Buy Price) CGT = Raw Profit × CGT Rate [only if Raw Profit > 0] Net Profit/Loss = Total Sell Revenue − CGT − Total Buy Cost Profit % = (Net Profit / Total Buy Cost) × 100

Real Examples with Full Calculation

Example 1 — Short-Term Profit (7.5% CGT)

Bought 100 shares at Rs. 800. Sold at Rs. 1,000 after 6 months (short-term).

STEP 1 — BUY SIDE: Shares = 100 Buy Price = Rs. 800 Buy Amount = 100 × Rs. 800 = Rs. 80,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 80,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 264 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 80,000 × 0.015% = Rs. 12 Total Buy Cost = Rs. 80,000 + Rs. 264 + Rs. 12 = Rs. 80,276 STEP 2 — SELL SIDE: Sell Price = Rs. 1,000 Sell Amount = 100 × Rs. 1,000 = Rs. 1,00,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 1,00,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 330 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 1,00,000 × 0.015%= Rs. 15 DP Charge = Rs. 25 Net Sell Revenue = Rs. 1,00,000 − Rs. 330 − Rs. 15 − Rs. 25 = Rs. 99,630 STEP 3 — RAW PROFIT (for CGT): Raw Profit = Rs. 1,00,000 − Rs. 80,000 = Rs. 20,000 STEP 4 — CGT (Short-term 7.5%): CGT = Rs. 20,000 × 7.5% = Rs. 1,500 STEP 5 — NET PROFIT: Net Profit = Rs. 99,630 − Rs. 1,500 − Rs. 80,276 = Rs. 17,854 Profit % = (Rs. 17,854 / Rs. 80,276) × 100 = 22.24%

Example 2 — Long-Term Profit (5% CGT)

Bought 200 shares at Rs. 500. Sold at Rs. 750 after 14 months (long-term).

STEP 1 — BUY SIDE: Buy Amount = 200 × Rs. 500 = Rs. 1,00,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 1,00,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 330 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 1,00,000 × 0.015%= Rs. 15 Total Buy Cost = Rs. 1,00,000 + Rs. 330 + Rs. 15 = Rs. 1,00,345 STEP 2 — SELL SIDE: Sell Amount = 200 × Rs. 750 = Rs. 1,50,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 1,50,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 495 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 1,50,000 × 0.015%= Rs. 22.50 DP Charge = Rs. 25 Net Sell Revenue = Rs. 1,50,000 − Rs. 495 − Rs. 22.50 − Rs. 25 = Rs. 1,49,457.50 STEP 3 — RAW PROFIT: Raw Profit = Rs. 1,50,000 − Rs. 1,00,000 = Rs. 50,000 STEP 4 — CGT (Long-term 5%): CGT = Rs. 50,000 × 5% = Rs. 2,500 STEP 5 — NET PROFIT: Net Profit = Rs. 1,49,457.50 − Rs. 2,500 − Rs. 1,00,345 = Rs. 46,612.50 Profit % = (Rs. 46,612.50 / Rs. 1,00,345) × 100 = 46.45%

Example 3 — Selling at a Loss (No CGT)

Bought 150 shares at Rs. 900. Sold at Rs. 700 (loss).

BUY SIDE: Buy Amount = 150 × Rs. 900 = Rs. 1,35,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 1,35,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 445.50 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 1,35,000 × 0.015%= Rs. 20.25 Total Buy Cost = Rs. 1,35,465.75 SELL SIDE: Sell Amount = 150 × Rs. 700 = Rs. 1,05,000 Brokerage (0.33%) = Rs. 1,05,000 × 0.33% = Rs. 346.50 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs. 1,05,000 × 0.015%= Rs. 15.75 DP Charge = Rs. 25 Net Sell Revenue = Rs. 1,05,000 − Rs. 346.50 − Rs. 15.75 − Rs. 25 = Rs. 1,04,612.75 RAW PROFIT = Rs. 1,05,000 − Rs. 1,35,000 = −Rs. 30,000 (LOSS) CGT = Rs. 0 (no tax on loss) NET LOSS = Rs. 1,04,612.75 − Rs. 1,35,465.75 = −Rs. 30,853

Most Common Mistakes When Calculating Profit

  • ❌ Forgetting brokerage on the buy side — most people only count sell brokerage
  • ❌ Ignoring SEBON fee — small but applies on both buy and sell
  • ❌ Forgetting DP charge — Rs. 25 per script on every sell
  • ❌ Calculating CGT on full sell amount — CGT is only on profit, not total amount
  • ❌ Using wrong CGT rate — must check exact holding period (365 days rule)
  • ❌ Not counting brokerage in buy cost for CGT base — raw profit for CGT is sell amount minus buy amount only, not including charges

Break-Even Price Formula

Want to know at what price you need to sell just to break even (zero profit)? Use this formula:

Break-Even Sell Price = (Total Buy Cost + DP Charge) ÷ (Shares × (1 − Brokerage% − SEBON%)) Simple Approximation: Break-Even Price ≈ Buy Price × 1.007 (adds ~0.7% to cover all charges at 0.33% tier) Example: Buy Price = Rs. 500 Break-Even ≈ Rs. 500 × 1.007 = Rs. 503.50

💡 Rule of Thumb: You need to sell at approximately 0.7% above your buy price just to break even after all charges (at the 0.33% brokerage tier).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Is profit calculated on buy price or buy cost?

For CGT purposes, raw profit = Sell Amount − Buy Amount (price × shares only). But your actual net profit also deducts all charges (brokerage, SEBON, DP, CGT) from both sides.

Q2: Do I pay CGT if I make a small profit of Rs. 500?

Yes. CGT applies on any profit, no matter how small. If your raw profit is Rs. 500 and it's short-term, CGT = Rs. 500 × 7.5% = Rs. 37.50.

Q3: How do I calculate profit for multiple lots bought at different prices?

Use the WACC (Weighted Average Cost) method. Add up all buy amounts and divide by total shares to get your average buy price. Then use that average price in the profit formula.

Q4: Is DP charge per share or per transaction?

Rs. 25 is per script (company) per day. Selling 1,000 shares of one company = Rs. 25 DP charge. Selling shares of 3 different companies in one day = Rs. 75 total DP charge.

Q5: How is profit calculated on bonus shares?

For bonus shares, the buy price is considered Rs. 100 (face value) since you received them for free. Holding period starts from the date you received the bonus shares.

Q6: Can I offset my loss in one stock against profit in another?

No. Since May 2026, CGT is a final tax. Each transaction is treated independently. Losses in one stock cannot be offset against gains in another for tax purposes.
