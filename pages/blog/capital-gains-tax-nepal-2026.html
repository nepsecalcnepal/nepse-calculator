Capital Gains Tax (CGT) on NEPSE Shares Nepal 2026: Complete Guide

📅 Published: June 1, 2026 ⏱️ Reading Time: 10 minutes 👤 NEPSE Calculator Nepal 📧 nepsecalcnepal@gmail.com

What is Capital Gains Tax on NEPSE Shares?

Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is the tax you pay on the profit earned when you sell shares at a higher price than you bought them from the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE). It is charged only on the gain, not on the full sale amount.

If you sell shares at a loss, you don't pay any CGT. The tax is automatically deducted by your stock broker at the time of selling through the TMS (Trading Management System) platform.

💡 Key Point: CGT is calculated on profit only, not the total selling amount. If you sell at a loss, CGT = Rs 0.

CGT Rates for NEPSE Shares 2026

The CGT rate depends entirely on how long you held the shares before selling them:

Holding Period Tax Rate Who It Applies To Example
Short-term (< 365 days) 7.5% Individual investors Buy Jan 2025, Sell Dec 2025
Long-term (≥ 365 days) 5% Individual investors Buy Jan 2024, Sell Feb 2025
Institutional 10% Companies, Mutual Funds All holding periods

⚠️ Important: The 365 days rule is exact. 364 days = short-term (7.5%). 365 days or more = long-term (5%).

🚨 NEW (May 2026): CGT is Now FINAL TAX

✅ Major Update: The Government of Nepal announced in May 2026 that Capital Gains Tax on listed NEPSE shares is now a FINAL TAX.

What this means for you:

  • ✅ You don't need to declare CGT in your annual income tax return
  • ✅ Tax is fully settled when your broker deducts it
  • ✅ No additional tax or refund applies
  • ✅ No separate filing needed
  • ⚠️ Applies only to listed NEPSE shares — NOT land, houses, or unlisted company shares

How to Calculate Capital Gains Tax (Step-by-Step)

Basic Formula:

Capital Gain = Selling Price − Cost Price CGT = Capital Gain × Tax Rate (7.5% or 5%)

Full Calculation Formula (All Charges):

— BUY SIDE — Buy Amount = Shares × Buy Price Brokerage (Buy)= Buy Amount × Brokerage Rate SEBON (Buy) = Buy Amount × 0.015% Total Buy Cost = Buy Amount + Brokerage + SEBON — SELL SIDE — Sell Amount = Shares × Sell Price Brokerage (Sell) = Sell Amount × Brokerage Rate SEBON (Sell) = Sell Amount × 0.015% DP Charge = Rs 25 (flat) Profit = Sell Amount − Total Buy Cost CGT = Profit × CGT Rate (only if Profit > 0) Net Sell Revenue = Sell Amount − Brokerage − SEBON − DP − CGT — RESULT — Net Profit/Loss = Net Sell Revenue − Total Buy Cost

Real Calculation Examples

Example 1 — Long-term Investment (5% CGT)

You bought 100 shares at Rs 500 in January 2024 and sold at Rs 700 in February 2025 (13 months = long-term).

BUY SIDE: Buy Amount = 100 × Rs 500 = Rs 50,000 Brokerage (0.36%)= Rs 50,000 × 0.36% = Rs 180 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs 50,000 × 0.015% = Rs 7.50 Total Buy Cost = Rs 50,000 + 180 + 7.50 = Rs 50,187.50 SELL SIDE: Sell Amount = 100 × Rs 700 = Rs 70,000 Brokerage (0.36%)= Rs 70,000 × 0.36% = Rs 252 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs 70,000 × 0.015% = Rs 10.50 DP Charge = Rs 25 Profit = Rs 70,000 − Rs 50,000 = Rs 20,000 CGT (5%) = Rs 20,000 × 5% = Rs 1,000 Net Sell Revenue= Rs 70,000 − 252 − 10.50 − 25 − 1,000 = Rs 68,712.50 NET PROFIT = Rs 68,712.50 − Rs 50,187.50 = Rs 18,525 PROFIT % = 36.91%

Example 2 — Short-term Trading (7.5% CGT)

You bought 200 shares at Rs 1,000 in June 2025 and sold at Rs 1,200 in February 2026 (8 months = short-term).

BUY SIDE: Buy Amount = 200 × Rs 1,000 = Rs 2,00,000 Brokerage (0.31%)= Rs 2,00,000 × 0.31% = Rs 620 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs 2,00,000 × 0.015% = Rs 30 Total Buy Cost = Rs 2,00,650 SELL SIDE: Sell Amount = 200 × Rs 1,200 = Rs 2,40,000 Brokerage (0.33%)= Rs 2,40,000 × 0.33% = Rs 792 SEBON (0.015%) = Rs 2,40,000 × 0.015% = Rs 36 DP Charge = Rs 25 Profit = Rs 2,40,000 − Rs 2,00,000 = Rs 40,000 CGT (7.5%) = Rs 40,000 × 7.5% = Rs 3,000 Net Sell Revenue= Rs 2,40,000 − 792 − 36 − 25 − 3,000 = Rs 2,36,147 NET PROFIT = Rs 2,36,147 − Rs 2,00,650 = Rs 35,497 PROFIT % = 17.7%

Example 3 — Selling at Loss (No CGT)

You bought 150 shares at Rs 800 and sold at Rs 700.

Buy Amount = 150 × Rs 800 = Rs 1,20,000 Sell Amount = 150 × Rs 700 = Rs 1,05,000 Loss = −Rs 15,000 CGT = Rs 0 (No tax on loss!)

How is CGT Collected?

  1. You sell shares through TMS (Trading Management System)
  2. Broker automatically calculates your profit/loss
  3. CGT is deducted from sell proceeds before depositing to your bank
  4. CGT amount visible in your transaction statement
  5. No separate payment or filing required (since May 2026)

CGT on Bonus Shares

For bonus shares, the holding period starts from the date you received the bonus shares, not the original purchase date:

  • Sold after 365 days → 5% CGT (individual, long-term)
  • Sold before 365 days → 7.5% CGT (individual, short-term)
  • Institutional investors → 10% CGT (all periods)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Do I pay CGT if I sell at a loss?

No! CGT is only charged on profit. If you sell at a loss, you pay Rs 0 CGT.

Q2: How many days is considered long-term?

365 days or more is long-term (5% CGT). 364 days or less is short-term (7.5%).

Q3: Do I need to file CGT in income tax?

No! Since May 2026, CGT on NEPSE shares is FINAL TAX. Broker deducts it automatically — no separate declaration needed.

Q4: Is CGT charged on IPO applications?

No, CGT is only charged when you sell in the secondary market (NEPSE). IPO applications have no CGT.

Q5: What happens with CGT in a merger/acquisition?

No immediate tax liability. The holding period carries forward to the new shares and CGT is calculated when you sell the new shares.

Q6: How is holding period calculated for bonus shares?

From the date you received bonus shares in your Demat account, NOT from the original share purchase date.

Q7: Can I offset CGT losses against other income?

No. Since CGT is final tax (May 2026), losses cannot be carried forward or offset. If you have a loss, no CGT is charged at all.
